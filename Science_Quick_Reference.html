Astronomy & Cosmology

TopicsUniverse · Galaxies · Sun · Moon · Planets
Age of the universe
~13.8 billion years
Observable universe
~93 billion light-years across
Galaxies
~200 billion to 2 trillion in the observable universe (Hubble 2016: ~2 trillion; New Horizons 2021: ~200 billion)
Stars per galaxy
~100 billion on average; from a few hundred (ultra-faint dwarfs) to ~100 trillion (giant ellipticals like IC 1101); Milky Way ~100 – 400 billion
Stars in the universe
~10²² to 10²⁴ (10 sextillion to 1 septillion) in the observable universe
Light-year
~9.46 trillion km, the distance light travels in one year
Sun
~150 million km from Earth = 1 AU (astronomical unit); light takes ~8 min 20 s
Moon
~384,400 km from Earth (~400,000 km); light takes ~1.3 s
Planets (from the Sun)
Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune; Pluto is a dwarf planet (since 2006)

Earth

TopicsSize · Motion · Layers · Surface · Water · Air · People
Age
~4.54 billion years
Diameter
~12,742 km (7,918 mi)
Circumference
~40,075 km (24,901 mi) around the equator
Day / year
24 h (exact rotation 23 h 56 min); 365.25 days, hence leap years
Axial tilt
23.4°, the cause of the seasons
Speeds
Spins ~1,670 km/h at the equator; orbits the Sun at ~107,000 km/h (30 km/s)
Layers
  • Crust: 5 – 70 km thick
  • Mantle: ~2,900 km thick
  • Outer core: liquid iron-nickel, ~6,960 km diameter
  • Inner core: solid iron-nickel, ~2,440 km diameter
Surface area
~510 million km²: 71% water, 29% land (~149 million km²)
Continents (7)
Asia, Africa, North America, South America, Antarctica, Europe, Australia (largest to smallest)
Oceans (5)
Pacific, Atlantic, Indian, Southern, Arctic (largest to smallest); the Pacific covers ~1/3 of Earth's surface
Highest / deepest
Mount Everest 8,849 m; Mariana Trench (Challenger Deep) ~10,935 m
Water by mass
Oceans are only ~0.02% of Earth's total mass; the mantle may hold several more oceans' worth locked in rock (estimates vary)
Fresh water
Only ~2.5% of Earth's water, mostly frozen in ice caps and glaciers
Atmosphere
78% nitrogen, 21% oxygen, ~1% argon; CO₂ ~0.04% (~425 ppm)
Average temperature
~15 °C (59 °F) at the surface
Human population
~117 billion people ever born (PRB estimate, ~110 – 120 billion depending on assumptions); ~8.2 billion alive today

Temperatures

TopicsAbsolute zero · Water · Earth · Sun · Fusion · CERN
Absolute zero
−273.15 °C (0 K, −459.67 °F), the coldest possible
Water
Freezes at 0 °C (32 °F); boils at 100 °C (212 °F) at sea level
Earth's core
~5,500 °C, about as hot as the Sun's surface
Sun's surface
~5,500 °C
Sun's core
~15 million °C
Fusion reactor
~100 – 200 million °C (plasma, about 10 times hotter than the Sun's core)
CERN collider (LHC)
~5 trillion °C (5,000 billion) in lead-ion collisions, the hottest temperature ever made by humans

Weather

TopicsCoriolis · Pressure · Heat dome
Coriolis effect
Northern Hemisphere storms (hurricanes) spin counterclockwise; Southern Hemisphere storms spin clockwise
Low pressure
Rising air, spiraling inward (counterclockwise in the NH); clouds and precipitation
High pressure
Sinking air, spiraling outward (clockwise in the NH); clear, stable weather
Heat dome
A persistent high-pressure ridge through the troposphere (~10 km), often under a stalled bulge in the jet stream
How a heat dome works
  • Sinking air compresses and warms.
  • No clouds, so more solar heating.
  • The ridge acts as a lid, trapping the heat.
