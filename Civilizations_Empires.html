Major Civilizations

The 18 civilizations and empires that most shaped world history, in chronological order, with their period, territorial extent at peak, and estimated population.

Sumer

Periodc. 4500 – 1900 BCE
Extent—
Population~1 million people; Uruk ~50,000

Located in southern Mesopotamia (modern Iraq), they invented the first writing system, cuneiform, and built the earliest city-states, such as Uruk.

Ancient Egypt

Periodc. 3100 – 30 BCE
Extent~1 million km² at peak
Population~3–5 million people

Emerging along the Nile River, this centralized civilization is famous for its pyramids, pharaohs, and hieroglyphic writing.

Indus Valley Civilization

Periodc. 2600 – 1900 BCE
Extent~1 million km²
Population~5 million people

Spanning modern Pakistan and northwest India, they pioneered grid-planned cities and advanced drainage and plumbing systems.

The Maya Civilization

Periodc. 2000 BCE – 1697 CE, peak c. 250 – 900 CE
Extent~325,000 km²
Population~7–10 million people

Centered in the rainforests of Mesoamerica, they excelled in mathematics, astronomy, calendar systems, hieroglyphic writing, and stepped pyramids.

Carthage

Periodc. 814 – 146 BCE
Extent~300,000 km²
Population—

Founded by Phoenician colonists in modern Tunisia, this maritime power dominated western Mediterranean trade until Rome destroyed it in the Punic Wars.

Ancient Rome

Periodc. 753 BCE – 476 CE
Extent~5 million km²
Population~60–70 million people

Growing from a kingdom into a republic and then an empire (27 BCE), Rome left a lasting legacy in law, engineering, roads, and architecture across Europe, North Africa, and the Middle East.

Achaemenid Persian Empire

Periodc. 550 – 330 BCE
Extent~5.5 million km²
Population~20–35 million people

Founded by Cyrus the Great, it stretched from Europe to India and pioneered the satrapy (provincial governor) system and the Royal Road.

Classical Greece

Periodc. 500 – 323 BCE
Extent1,000+ city-states
PopulationAthens/Attica ~250,000 people

Centered on city-states like Athens and Sparta, it produced foundational advances in philosophy, democracy, mathematics, and theater.

Wider Greek World
c. 800 – 146 BCE, from the Archaic period to the Roman conquest.
Legacy
  • Governance: foundations of Western political systems, including democracy in Athens.
  • Philosophy: critical inquiry established by Socrates, Plato, and Aristotle.
  • Science & Math: fundamental principles developed by Pythagoras, Euclid, and Archimedes.
  • Culture: lasting innovations in drama, epic literature, architecture, and sculpture.

The Han Dynasty

Period202 BCE – 220 CE
Extent~6 million km²
Population~58 million people, 2 CE census

Setting the blueprint for later Chinese history, it opened the Silk Road, adopted Confucianism as state philosophy, and invented paper.

The Byzantine Empire

Period330 – 1453 CE
Extent~2.7 million km² at peak, 555 CE
Population—

The Greek-speaking, Christian eastern half of the Roman Empire preserved Roman law, built the Hagia Sophia, and lasted over a thousand years.

The Islamic Caliphate

Period632 – 1258 CE
Extent~11 million km² at peak, Umayyad era
Population—

Expanding from Arabia, it unified North Africa, the Middle East, and parts of Europe, launching the Islamic Golden Age of science and scholarship.

Mongol Empire

Period1206 – 1368 CE
Extent~24 million km², largest contiguous land empire
Population—

Founded by Genghis Khan, it linked Europe and Asia and revived Silk Road trade.

The Ottoman Empire

Period1299 – 1922 CE
Extent~5.2 million km² at peak, 1683
Population—

Spanning Europe, Asia, and Africa after taking Constantinople in 1453, this Islamic superpower dominated Mediterranean trade and ran a highly organized state.

The Ming Dynasty

Period1368 – 1644 CE
Extent~6.5 million km²
Population~160 million people

Ending Mongol rule in China, it built the Forbidden City, rebuilt the Great Wall, and launched Zheng He's treasure voyages.

The Aztec Empire

Period1428 – 1521 CE
Extent~200,000 km²
Population~5–6 million people; Tenochtitlan ~200,000

Formed by a triple alliance in the Valley of Mexico, it built the island capital of Tenochtitlan and a vast tribute empire supported by advanced agriculture.

The Inca Empire

Period1438 – 1572 CE
Extent~2 million km²
Population~10–12 million people

Stretching along the Andes, the largest empire in pre-Columbian America was unified by a vast road system, precise stone masonry, and quipu record-keeping.

Andean Context
The last and largest of a 5,000-year line of Andean civilizations. See Spotlight: Andean Civilizations.

The British Empire

Periodc. 1600 – 1997 CE
Extent~35 million km²
Population~410+ million people c. 1920

Built on maritime trade and colonies across the Americas, Africa, Asia, and Oceania, it became the largest empire in history and spread the Industrial Revolution worldwide.

The Russian Empire

Period1721 – 1917 CE
Extent~22.8 million km²
Population~175 million people c. 1914

Expanding from the Tsardom of Russia into a transcontinental empire across Europe, Asia, and briefly North America (Alaska), it westernized its state and military under Peter the Great.

Spotlight: Andean Civilizations

Beginning around 3500 – 3000 BCE, the Andean region was, alongside Mesoamerica, one of the few areas in the Americas where complex urban civilization developed independently. Over nearly 5,000 years, a sequence of cultures built on one another, ending with the Inca Empire and the Spanish conquest.

Preceramic & Early Horizon c. 3500 – 200 BCE

Caral-Supe (Norte Chico)

Periodc. 3500 – 1800 BCE
RegionCentral coast of Peru
Key SiteCaral

The oldest known complex society in the Americas. They built massive monumental mounds and sunken plazas, predating the widespread use of ceramics.

Chavín Culture

Periodc. 900 – 200 BCE
RegionPeruvian highlands
Key SiteChavín de Huántar

A widespread religious and artistic horizon, known for stone iconography and underground temples.

Early Intermediate Period c. 200 BCE – 600 CE

Moche

Periodc. 100 – 700 CE
RegionNorthern coast of Peru
Key SiteHuaca del Sol & Huaca de la Luna

Master metallurgists and builders who constructed the massive adobe pyramids Huaca del Sol and Huaca de la Luna.

Nazca

Periodc. 100 BCE – 800 CE
RegionSouthern coast of Peru
Key SiteNazca Lines

Famed for their extensive geoglyphs (the Nazca Lines) cut into the arid desert floor and sophisticated polychrome pottery.

Middle Horizon c. 600 – 1000 CE

Wari (Huari) Empire

Periodc. 600 – 1000 CE
RegionCentral highlands of Peru
Key SiteHuari

A heavily urbanized expansionist state that established road networks and administrative centers.

Tiwanaku (Tiahuanaco)

Periodc. 600 – 1000 CE
RegionLake Titicaca, Bolivia
Key SiteTiwanaku

A major ceremonial city-state renowned for stone masonry and high-altitude terrace farming.

Late Intermediate Period & Late Horizon c. 1000 – 1532 CE

Chimú Empire

Periodc. 1000 – 1470 CE
RegionNorthern coast of Peru
Key SiteChan Chan

Built the massive adobe city of Chan Chan before being conquered by the Inca.

Inca Empire (Tawantinsuyu)

Period1438 – 1532 CE
RegionEntire Andean region
Key SiteCusco & Machu Picchu

The largest empire in pre-Columbian America, uniting the Andes through the Qhapaq Ñan highway system, terrace agriculture, administrative centers such as Cusco and Machu Picchu, and quipu record-keeping, until the Spanish conquest.

Shared Cultural Innovations

Despite diverse landscapes, from hyper-arid coastal deserts to high-altitude mountain valleys, Andean societies shared several fundamental innovations:

Agricultural Mastery

High-altitude crop varieties (potatoes, quinoa, maize) grown on stone terraces (andenes) with complex irrigation canals.

Textiles & Fiber Arts

Highly intricate weaving in cotton and camelid wool (llama and alpaca), with immense religious, social, and economic value.

Quipu

A record-keeping system of knotted, color-coded strings used for accounting, census data, and administrative coordination.

Monumental Stone Masonry

Dry-stone construction engineered to withstand the region's frequent earthquakes.
