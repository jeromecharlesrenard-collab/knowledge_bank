Civilizations & Empires

Major and other civilizations and empires through history: periods, size, population, and legacy.

Major Civilizations

The 17 civilizations and empires that most shaped world history, in chronological order, with their period, territorial extent at peak, and estimated population.

1

Sumer

Periodc. 4500 – 1900 BCE
Extent—
Population~1 million people; Uruk ~50,000

Located in southern Mesopotamia (modern Iraq), they invented the first writing system, cuneiform, and built the earliest city-states, such as Uruk.

2

Ancient Egypt

Periodc. 3100 – 30 BCE
Extent~1 million km² at peak
Population~3–5 million people

Emerging along the Nile River, this centralized civilization is famous for its pyramids, pharaohs, and hieroglyphic writing.

3

Indus Valley Civilization

Periodc. 2600 – 1900 BCE
Extent~1 million km²
Population~5 million people

Spanning modern Pakistan and northwest India, they pioneered grid-planned cities and advanced drainage and plumbing systems.

4

The Maya Civilization

Periodc. 2000 BCE – 1697 CE, peak c. 250 – 900 CE
Extent~325,000 km²
Population~7–10 million people

Centered in the rainforests of Mesoamerica, they excelled in mathematics, astronomy, calendar systems, hieroglyphic writing, and stepped pyramids.

5

Ancient Rome

Periodc. 753 BCE – 476 CE
Extent~5 million km²
Population~60–70 million people

Growing from a kingdom into a republic and then an empire (27 BCE), Rome left a lasting legacy in law, engineering, roads, and architecture across Europe, North Africa, and the Middle East.

6

Achaemenid Persian Empire

Periodc. 550 – 330 BCE
Extent~5.5 million km²
Population~20–35 million people

Founded by Cyrus the Great, it stretched from Europe to India and pioneered the satrapy (provincial governor) system and the Royal Road.

7

Classical Greece

Periodc. 500 – 323 BCE
Extent1,000+ city-states
PopulationAthens/Attica ~250,000 people

Centered on city-states like Athens and Sparta, it produced foundational advances in philosophy, democracy, mathematics, and theater.

8

The Han Dynasty

Period202 BCE – 220 CE
Extent~6 million km²
Population~58 million people, 2 CE census

Setting the blueprint for later Chinese history, it opened the Silk Road, adopted Confucianism as state philosophy, and invented paper.

9

The Byzantine Empire

Period330 – 1453 CE
Extent~2.7 million km² at peak, 555 CE
Population—

The Greek-speaking, Christian eastern half of the Roman Empire preserved Roman law, built the Hagia Sophia, and lasted over a thousand years.

10

The Islamic Caliphate

Period632 – 1258 CE
Extent~11 million km² at peak, Umayyad era
Population—

Expanding from Arabia, it unified North Africa, the Middle East, and parts of Europe, launching the Islamic Golden Age of science and scholarship.

11

Mongol Empire

Period1206 – 1368 CE
Extent~24 million km², largest contiguous land empire
Population—

Founded by Genghis Khan, it linked Europe and Asia and revived Silk Road trade.

12

The Ottoman Empire

Period1299 – 1922 CE
Extent~5.2 million km² at peak, 1683
Population—

Spanning Europe, Asia, and Africa after taking Constantinople in 1453, this Islamic superpower dominated Mediterranean trade and ran a highly organized state.

13

The Ming Dynasty

Period1368 – 1644 CE
Extent~6.5 million km²
Population~160 million people

Ending Mongol rule in China, it built the Forbidden City, rebuilt the Great Wall, and launched Zheng He's treasure voyages.

14

The Aztec Empire

Period1428 – 1521 CE
Extent~200,000 km²
Population~5–6 million people; Tenochtitlan ~200,000

Formed by a triple alliance in the Valley of Mexico, it built the island capital of Tenochtitlan and a vast tribute empire supported by advanced agriculture.

15

The Inca Empire

Period1438 – 1572 CE
Extent~2 million km²
Population~10–12 million people

Stretching along the Andes, the largest empire in pre-Columbian America was unified by a vast road system, precise stone masonry, and quipu record-keeping.

16

The British Empire

Periodc. 1600 – 1997 CE
Extent~35 million km²
Population~410+ million people c. 1920

Built on maritime trade and colonies across the Americas, Africa, Asia, and Oceania, it became the largest empire in history and spread the Industrial Revolution worldwide.

17

The Russian Empire

Period1721 – 1917 CE
Extent~22.8 million km²
Population~175 million people c. 1914

Expanding from the Tsardom of Russia into a transcontinental empire across Europe, Asia, and briefly North America (Alaska), it westernized its state and military under Peter the Great.
