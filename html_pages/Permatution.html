Permatution

How many ways people or cards can be arranged: factorial counts for lines, round tables, and rectangular tables.

A permutation is one specific order of a set of distinct items. Click a box to open it.

The Basic Rule

TopicsLine vs. circle
Fixed line of seats
With n distinct seats (a bench, a row, a jury box), every seat is a fixed position, so there are n! (n factorial) possible arrangements. Two rows of fixed seats count the same way: it is still n! as long as each of the n seats is a distinct position.
Round table
At a round table there is no fixed starting seat. Rotating everyone around the table by one or more places turns one arrangement into another that looks identical, so each arrangement is counted n times in n!. Dividing it out leaves (n−1)! distinct arrangements.
Mirror images
If a clockwise order and its mirrored, counterclockwise order are also treated as the same arrangement, divide by 2 again: (n−1)!/2 for a round table, n!/2 for a line.

Six People Around a Table

TopicsRound vs. line, worked small example
Round table
(6−1)! = 5! = 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 120 arrangements
Line of 6 seats
6! = 6 × 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 720 arrangements
Why the difference
The line has a fixed left end and right end, so every rotation is a new arrangement; the round table treats all 6 rotations of the same order as one, so 720 ÷ 6 = 120.

Twelve People: Jury, Bench, and Round Table

Topics12! vs. 11!, mirror images
12 jurors in 1 or 2 rows
Jury seats are fixed positions (it does not matter whether they sit in one row or split across two), so the count is 12! = 479,001,600, about 480 million arrangements.
12 people on a long bench
Same logic as the jury: a bench has a fixed left end and right end, so every seat is distinct. 12! = 479,001,600 arrangements.
12 people around a round table
No fixed starting seat, so rotations of the same order count once: (12−1)! = 11! = 39,916,800, about 39 million arrangements — 12 times fewer than the bench, since each round-table arrangement corresponds to 12 rotated bench arrangements.
Round table, mirror images merged
If clockwise and counterclockwise seatings around the table are treated as identical, divide by 2 again: 11!/2 = 19,958,400 arrangements.

Twelve People Around a Rectangular Table

TopicsSix per side, partial symmetry
Setup
12 people, six seats on each of the two long sides.
Seats treated as fixed (each chair distinct)
12! = 479,001,600 — the same count as the bench, since every chair is a distinct, nameable position.
Arrangements equal under rotation
12!/2 = 239,500,800. A rectangle looks the same again only after a 180° turn (unlike a round table, which looks the same after any of 12 rotations), so each arrangement is counted twice rather than 12 times.
Mirror images also treated as equal
12!/4 = 119,750,400, which additionally accounts for flipping the table lengthwise or widthwise.
Standard answer
12!/2 = 239,500,800. The rectangle's corners and distinct long/short sides break most of the circular symmetry a round table has, so the rectangle's count lands much closer to the bench's 12! than to the round table's 11!.

How Big Is a Permutation Number?

TopicsDeck of cards, atoms in the universe
Standard deck of 52 cards
The number of possible orderings is 52! ≈ 8.07 × 10⁶⁷ (commonly rounded to about 10⁶⁸).
Atoms in the observable universe
Generally estimated at 10⁷⁸ to 10⁸² atoms.
Putting it together
52! is far larger than the 12-person seating counts above, yet still about 10¹⁰ to 10¹⁴ times smaller than the estimated number of atoms in the observable universe. It is nonetheless so large that any thoroughly shuffled deck is almost certainly a specific order that has never existed before in the history of the universe.
