Dinausor

Dinosaur timeline: Triassic origins, Jurassic giants, Cretaceous peak diversity, the K-Pg extinction, and birds as living dinosaurs.

Dinosaurs ruled Earth for over 165 million years, from the Triassic to the end of the Cretaceous. Click a box to open it. Dates marked ~ are approximate.

Triassic Period

Span252 – 201 Ma
~237 – 227 Ma
The earliest dinosaurs emerge in the Late Triassic: small, swift, bipedal carnivores or omnivores such as Eoraptor and Herrerasaurus
Early split
Dinosaurs diverge into two main lineages: Saurischians ("lizard-hipped," ancestors of theropods and sauropodomorphs) and Ornithischians ("bird-hipped," ancestors of armored and horned dinosaurs)
~201 Ma
End-Triassic extinction: massive volcanic eruptions (the Central Atlantic Magmatic Province) drive rapid climate change, wiping out major competing reptile groups and clearing the way for dinosaurs to dominate

Jurassic Period

Span201 – 145 Ma
The Age of Giants
Warm, humid climates and abundant vegetation drive huge evolutionary growth
Sauropods
Massive long-necked herbivores spread globally, such as Diplodocus and Brachiosaurus
Apex predators
Allosaurus and Ceratosaurus, alongside armored herbivores such as Stegosaurus
~150 Ma
Small, feathered theropod dinosaurs begin evolving into early birds, such as Archaeopteryx

Cretaceous Period

Span145 – 66 Ma
Peak diversity
Flowering plants (angiosperms) evolve, alongside diverse new dinosaur families
Ceratopsians & hadrosaurs
Triceratops and Edmontosaurus
Armored dinosaurs
Ankylosaurus
Apex theropods
Tyrannosaurus rex, Spinosaurus, and Velociraptor
Continental drift
Continents drift further apart, producing distinct regional dinosaur species across North America, South America, Asia, and Africa

K-Pg Extinction Event

When66 Ma
The asteroid impact
A ~10-km (6-mile) wide asteroid strikes near the Yucatan Peninsula, Mexico (the Chicxulub crater), causing massive wildfires, global dust and soot clouds, rapid ocean acidification, and severe prolonged cooling (an "impact winter")
Mass extinction
~75% of all species on Earth perish, including all non-avian dinosaurs

Post-Extinction / Cenozoic Era

Span66 Ma – present
Avian survival
A single theropod lineage survives the extinction: avian dinosaurs, i.e. birds
Modern successors
Modern birds are the surviving clade of theropod dinosaurs, so the dinosaur lineage continues to thrive today across thousands of species, from raptors and songbirds to waterfowl
